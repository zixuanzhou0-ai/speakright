# Pronunciation content and notation policy

Last reviewed: 2026-07-13
Accent scope: General American English

## Learning goal

SpeakRight teaches intelligibility, stable motor control, transfer, and retention.
It does not treat accent elimination or imitation of a single native-speaker
voice as the learning goal.

## Articulation language

- Vowel contrasts are described primarily by vowel quality, tongue position,
  lip posture, and tenseness. Duration is a contextual cue, not the definition
  of the contrast.
- General American /r/ accepts both common bunched and retroflex tongue shapes.
  The important shared property is a vocal-tract constriction without the
  tongue normally contacting the palate.
- Mandarin transfer patterns are hypotheses to test with the individual
  learner, never diagnoses inferred from group membership.

## Learner-facing notation

SpeakRight retains a learner-oriented broad transcription so existing content
and audio assets remain compatible:

- SpeakRight /e/ corresponds to /?/ in some dictionaries.
- SpeakRight /i?/ corresponds to /i/ in many General American dictionaries.
- SpeakRight /??r/ corresponds to /?/ in many American dictionaries.
- Source-specific chart notation may be preserved when an asset is explicitly
  presented as a source transcription.

## Source policy

Every English phoneme instruction is linked through the content review registry
to an articulation source, an IPA/notation source, the accent scope, the review
date, and review status. Primary references are the University of Iowa Sounds
of Speech, Cambridge Dictionary pronunciation symbols, and the International
Phonetic Association chart.

Automated scores remain observations. Content rules cannot turn a single score
into a substitution diagnosis, mastery claim, or long-term learner trait.
